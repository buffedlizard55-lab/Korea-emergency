In an emergency

Tap to call. Say “English please” when the operator answers — you will be connected to an interpreter. 112 and 119 work without an area code and are free from payphones.

119 Ambulance · Fire · Rescue Also sea/coast rescue. Auto-locates your phone. 112 Police Crime, theft, assault, feeling unsafe. 1330 Travel Helpline — English 24/7 Interpreting, help, complaints. Overseas +82‑2‑1330. +82‑2‑397‑4114 U.S. Embassy Seoul — 24/7 Lost passport, arrest, serious emergency.

⚠️ 1339 is not an ambulance

1339 is the KDCA infectious-disease line (24/7). Some tourism pages — including Seoul’s own — still call it an “Emergency Medical Information Center.” That wording is out of date.

For an ambulance, always call 119.

Sources: KDCA · VISITKOREA

⚠️ 122 no longer exists

The old maritime emergency number 122 was abolished and merged into 119. This matters at Haeundae, Gwangalli and Songdo.

If someone is in trouble in the water: call 119. Do not enter the water yourself.

Source: MOIS — integrated emergency reporting

Where you are matters

Different help is available in each city.

Seoul · Nov 1–9 & 15–22

🏙️ Seoul

U.S. Embassy is here. 188 Sejong-daero, Jongno-gu, Seoul 03141. Closed weekends & holidays, but the emergency line runs 24/7.

  • Tourist police patrol Myeong-dong, Itaewon and Dongdaemun — dial 1330 to find the nearest officer.
  • Police stations give free interpretation in an emergency.
  • Seoul Global Center 02‑2075‑4180 — help in many languages.
  • Hospitals with interpreters: Severance 02‑2228‑5800 · Seoul National University Hospital 02‑2072‑0505 · Samsung Medical Center 02‑3410‑0200. These desks are weekday-only — after hours use 119.
  • Crowd safety: Itaewon and Hongdae get dense on weekend nights. If you are being compressed, move to an edge or exit early.

Visit Seoul safety · Hospital list · State Dept

Busan · Nov 9–15

🌊 Busan

No U.S. consular services in Busan

The State Department states consular services, including passports, are only available at the U.S. Embassy in Seoul. A lost passport here means a trip back to Seoul — keep photocopies split between you.

  • 119 offers interpretation in 17 languages and auto-locates you.
  • 24-hour ER: Dong-A University Hospital, 26 Daesingongwon-ro, Seo-gu — 051‑240‑2000.
  • Multilingual: Pusan National University Hospital 051‑240‑7000. Near Haeundae: Inje Haeundae Paik Hospital 051‑797‑0566.
  • Foreign Residents Call Center 1600‑0051 — 9 languages, Sun–Fri 09:00–18:00.
  • Busan 120 — city help desk, weekdays 09:00–18:00, 6 languages (from outside Busan: +82‑51‑120, press 7).
  • Beaches in November: outside the lifeguarded season — no supervision.
  • Lost on the metro? Humetro lost & found, Seomyeon Station, 051‑640‑7339, weekdays 09:00–18:00. Report the same day.

✅ No Korean public holidays during your trip

Korea has 14 public holidays in 2026 and none of them falls in November, so no holiday-driven mass travel. CSAT on 19 Nov is not a public holiday — banks and offices stay open.

Install before you fly

These let you get help without speaking Korean.

The government’s app for foreign visitors: disaster alerts, safety guides and civil defense shelter locations. Seoul describes multilingual support in English, Chinese and Japanese across 36 services; KTO says action guidelines are available in 14 languages.

Report an emergency without making a phone call — pick a picture matching your situation, or send text and photos. English, Chinese, Japanese, Vietnamese and Filipino.

Call or live-chat with the Korea Travel Helpline. Voice is 24/7 in English; chat is 24/7 via the app, KakaoTalk, Messenger, LINE and WeChat.

Before you land

The few things that can actually stop you at the border.

📄 e-Arrival Card — required

You travel on the K-ETA exemption, so you must submit the e-Arrival Card. It is free, and filing opens 3 days before arrival (Korea time); a submission lapses 72 hours after you send it — so don’t do it too early.

Fake sites exist

The official portal warns that copycat sites are circulating. e-arrivalcard.go.kr is the only official site. Any site asking for payment is fake.

Open the official portal

🛂 Entry & customs

  • No visa needed for stays up to 90 days.
  • No K-ETA — the U.S. exemption runs through 31 Dec 2026.
  • Passport must be valid at entry; State says there is no minimum-validity rule (6 months is just a common precaution, not a Korean requirement).
  • Cash over USD 10,000 (both of you combined, any currency) must be declared — tick item 3 on the declaration form. The certificate cannot be issued once you leave the arrivals area. Penalties are severe.
  • Never bring cannabis, CBD or hemp in any form — including transit.

If something happens

SituationWhat to do
Medical emergency Call 119 and say “English please.” In Busan, 119 has interpreting in 17 languages and locates you automatically.
Lost / stolen passport Call the U.S. Embassy in Seoul, +82‑2‑397‑4114 (24/7) and file a police report at 112. Busan cannot help — services are Seoul only.
Crime, theft, feeling unsafe Call 112, or go to any police station — they provide free interpretation in emergencies.
Taxi overcharging / tourist scam Report to 1330 or the Tourist Complaint Center.
Can’t speak — need to report Use the Baro Emergency Reporting App (photo/text), or text and video-call 119.
Lost an item Busan metro: Seomyeon Station, 051‑640‑7339. Ask 1330 for help in English.
Earthquake Shaking usually lasts one to two minutes. Get under a sturdy table, protect your head, then secure an exit and turn off gas and electricity. Busan publishes guides in 12 languages.
Someone in the water Call 119 — not 122, which no longer exists. Don’t go in after them.
Emergency alert on your phone Alerts arrive in Korean with an English keyword on the top tiers. Check the Emergency Ready App or Safe Korea.
